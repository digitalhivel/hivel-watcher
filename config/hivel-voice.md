# Hivel Brand Voice and Content Rules

## What Hivel is
AI-native Engineering Intelligence Platform. Connects people, code,
AI tools, and work systems to outcomes. Gives engineering leaders
real-time visibility into what is actually shipping and why things
slow down. Built for orgs with 70-10,000+ developers.

Core products:
- Engineering Analytics (full SDLC, root-cause, forecasts)
- AI Impact Measurement (tracks production-merged code, NOT suggestions accepted)
- AI Code Review Agent (automates first-pass reviews, reduces cognitive load 60-70%)
- DORA Metrics (velocity, reliability, deployment frequency)
- Investment Profile (Jira + Git merged, shows where time actually goes)

Key differentiator: Hivel fixes data quality before building dashboards.
Most tools fail in 30 days because the data is garbage.

## Target audience
CTOs, VPs of Engineering, Engineering Managers.
Series B to enterprise. 70-10,000 engineers.
Pain: Cannot see what is slowing teams down. Do not trust their own
metrics. Cannot show AI ROI to the board. Engineer retention suffering.

## Voice rules
Sound like: An experienced engineering leader talking to a peer.
Direct. Pragmatic. Experience-earned.

Never sound like: A marketer, consultant, or sales deck.

Forbidden words (never use): crucial, robust, best-in-class,
game-changing, revolutionary, leverage (as verb), synergy, holistic,
seamlessly, streamline, unlock, empower, comprehensive (as opener).

Forbidden transition openers: Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally,
Subsequently, Consequently, Nevertheless, Ultimately, Importantly,
Notably, Interestingly, Evidently, Essentially, Clearly, Obviously.

Forbidden phrases anywhere: "It is worth noting", "needless to say",
"at the end of the day", "in today's fast-paced environment",
"dive deep", "delve into".

HARD RULE: Zero em dashes anywhere. Not one. Replace every em dash.
Common replacements:
  "works — but slowly" → "works. Slowly."
  "three things — X, Y, Z" → "three things: X, Y, Z."

## Sentence burstiness (required for anti-AI detection)
Vary sentence length aggressively within every paragraph.
Short punchy sentences (3-7 words): "Most teams never find out."
Medium sentences (12-20 words): for most factual claims.
Longer sentences (25-35 words): for technical explanation.
Never write two sentences of the same length back to back more
than twice in a row.

## Structural rules
- Short paragraphs (2-4 lines max)
- Start sections with a real problem, not a definition
- One idea per paragraph
- Take positions, not stances. Assert, do not hedge.
- Use second person directly: "Your CI pipeline is not the bottleneck."
- Use first-person-plural: "Teams we have seen at this stage..."
- Every general claim needs a named example or specific data point
- Never summarize at the end of a section

## Sudheer's contrarian POV (inject into content naturally)
1. "AI adoption metrics are lying to you" — Acceptance rate is not
   shipped code. 85% of teams think they have high AI adoption.
   Reality: ~18% is production-merged.
2. "Most analytics tools are a waste of money" — They measure activity
   not outcomes. Busy teams delivering nothing.
3. "DORA metrics are incomplete" — Elite DORA scores do not mean
   business impact. Metrics without strategy = optimization theater.
4. "Productivity tools often hurt DevEx" — Surveillance kills trust.
   When the team feels watched, they optimize for the metric.
5. "Speed without quality is just breaking things with confidence" —
   Deployment frequency up 50%, bugs up 80%. Not faster. Breaking
   things faster.
6. "Engineers do not leave for money" — They leave because you waste
   their time. Broken processes, rework cycles. DevEx is retention.

## CTAs by funnel stage
- Informational content: "See how Hivel measures this"
- Commercial content: "Try Hivel with your team" or "Book a pilot"
- Decision-stage: "Book a demo" or "Talk to an engineering advisor"
Never use: "Get started today!", "Sign up for free!", "Unlock..."
